How Timecast works

The weather as a picture: one circle, one sky and a few words. Everything on screen is drawn from the forecast for that place and hour. There are no photos and no icons.

01The circle

By day the circle is the sun, and at night it's a ring: the moon. The hotter it gets, the more the sun pulses and glows. Behind thick cloud or fog it dims and goes soft.

02The sky

The background mixes two colours in a diagonal gradient: one for the time of day where you are, one for the temperature.

Three things adjust it. At night the temperature colour sinks into the dark, so a mild night still looks like night. Clouds, fog, rain and snow take colour out of the sky. On very pale skies the text turns dark so it stays readable.

03The weather

The weather is drawn live on top of the sky: thin rain that slants in the wind, round snow, cloud bands across the circle, low fog, stars at night and thunder flashes.

04The timeline

The bars at the bottom are the next 24 hours. Drag across them (or hover on a computer) and the whole screen changes to that hour.

Now
  • Colour: each bar is a small copy of that hour's sky.
  • Height: the temperature, from the day's low to its high.
  • Ticks and dots: rain and snow, more marks for heavier.
  • Moon to sun: the night, from sunset to sunrise.

05Your places

  • Search any city. Pin the ones you check often and they appear first.
  • Your location: the weather where you are.
  • Random: a capital city somewhere in the world, to see another sky.

06Data & privacy

Forecasts come from Open-Meteo. City names come from BigDataCloud and Open-Meteo. Your location is only sent to them to get the forecast. Timecast has no server, no account and no tracking. Pinned places, your °C/°F choice and your last place are saved in this browser only.

07Tips

  • °Tap the temperature to switch between °C and °F.
  • +Add it to your home screen from your browser's share menu. It works offline with the last forecast.
